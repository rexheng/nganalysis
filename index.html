Berkeley endorsements, November 2026

What the network shows

Each dot is a candidate, person or organization; a line means one endorsed the other. Dots are placed by a force-directed layout, so actors who share endorsements sit near each other. Click a dot or search for a name to see its connections and sources.

    How often are two candidates grouped together?

    Share of clustering runs (percent) that put each pair in the same group. Outlined cells are pairs inside a robust coalition (every pair at least ). Rows and columns are ordered by coalition; unattached candidates come last.

    How much do endorser lists overlap?

    By default, shared endorsers as a percent of everyone who endorses either candidate (Jaccard similarity), so long lists do not dominate. Hover, tap or focus a cell for names.

    Built from public campaign websites, organization endorsement pages and the Alameda County Voter Information Guide. Every link in this graphic goes to its source; if a listing is wrong, the source page is the place to start.
